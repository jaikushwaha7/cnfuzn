# Methods by root question

## 1. What is true? (Bayesian evidence ledger)
1. State the claim in one sentence.
2. **Prior**: "Before looking at evidence, how likely is it, 0 to 100%?"
3. List evidence on **both** sides. For each: supports / contradicts; strength (weak ×1.5, moderate ×3, strong ×6); source reliability (shaky ^0.5, ok ^1, solid ^1.25).
4. Update: posterior odds = prior odds × each supporting factor (÷ for contradicting). Report the percent.
5. **Bias check**: if evidence is all on one side, ask for the strongest case against.
6. Count independent evidence only. Five articles repeating one source count once.
7. Ask: "What would change your mind?" and "What is the cheapest test that would settle it?"
Verdict: ≥70% leans true · ≤30% leans false · otherwise unresolved, so gather evidence rather than act on a coin flip about a fact.

## 2. What do I want? (Five Whys + emotion check)
1. "I think I want…" then ask "Why does that matter?" up to five times. The last answer is usually the real want.
2. Emotion check: intensity 0–10; "Would I still want this in a week?"; whose voice is this (mine / someone else's / both)?
3. If two feelings conflict, name each and what need it protects.
If intensity ≥ 7 or "probably not in a week": wait 24–72 hours before any irreversible move; do something small and reversible meanwhile.

## 3. What should I do? (ability-to-act lenses)
List 2–5 options ("wait" and "small version first" are valid). Rate each 0–10 on:
- **Easy to start**: a first step within 48 hours?
- **Uses my strengths**
- **Staying power** when it gets hard
- **Room to adjust**: reversible?
- **Fits my values**
- **Worth it either way**: learning even if it disappoints
- **Support & resources**
Score = mean × 10 (optionally double-weight two lenses the person says matter most). Rank. Within 6 points = a tie; break it by reversibility or which feels lighter.
If the best is below 50: nothing is easy to act on yet. Add a smaller option (2-week trial, one conversation) and re-rate.
For moral conflicts also ask: which two values collide, who is affected, and "would I be comfortable if this were public?" (No = a moral alarm to examine.)
Gut check: "If a coin decided, which result would you feel relieved by?" If it differs from the ranking, find the lens driving the gap.

## 4. Who am I? (values + inherited-vs-chosen)
1. Choose up to five values the person would defend even when costly.
2. For each identity layer in play (gender, race/ethnicity, caste, religion/belief, nationality, class, family expectations): what was received or expected, and is the person **keeping**, **questioning**, **releasing** or **undecided**?
3. Outside-pressure 0–10. If ≥ 7, note that answers may reflect others' voices; revisit when calmer.
4. Finish: "If nobody were watching or judging, I would…"
Never assign an identity or belief. Reflect back what they said. Identity isn't solved in one sitting: review in 30 days.

## 5. Where am I going? (PDSA)
1. **Plan**: the smallest real test that teaches something either way.
2. **Do**: when exactly.
3. **Check**: what will be measured or noticed.
4. **Act**: the decision rule for each result, written *before* seeing it.
Plan-vs-adapt: high-cost or hard-to-reverse → lean plan (cap planning at a date). Cheap and reversible → lean adapt (keep a check-in date).

## Related established methods
Scientific method and hypothesis testing; statistical inference; systematic review (prefer many sources over one anecdote); root cause analysis and fishbone diagrams; systems thinking (map interacting causes, find leverage points); PDCA/PDSA.
